About Me

Learning beyond spurious correlations

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Air Force Medical Center, Air Force Medical University. I received my Ph.D. in Computational Biophysics from the Department of Physics and Astronomy at the University of Manchester, supervised by Professor Henggui Zhang.

My research asks what information is identifiable from limited supervision, which structures remain stable under distribution shift, and when a model can be safely evaluated, calibrated, or updated. I address these questions through statistical learning theory, representation auditing, multimodal counterfactual evaluation, gradient geometry, clinical data analysis, and multiscale biophysical simulation.

A central motivation comes from biomedical systems: sparse cellular measurements must explain tissue-level arrhythmia, physiological signals must be interpreted across subjects and centers, and medical imaging models must generalize despite acquisition and institutional shortcuts.

Core research question Under partial observation, source heterogeneity, and distribution shift, which learned structures are identifiable and invariant rather than spurious—and what evidence is required to validate them?
  • Identifiable representation structure
  • Shortcut-resistant multimodal evaluation
  • Cross-center biomedical AI
  • Physiological signal dynamics
  • Gradient-based model intervention
  • Multiscale cardiac electrophysiology

Research Projects

Representative scientific programs

Project 01

Vestibular autonomic function stability in pilot candidates

Led a multidisciplinary, three-center study involving Coriolis acceleration testing and continuous ECG analysis. Developed a multi-domain HRV pipeline for dynamic physiological assessment across rest, stimulation, and recovery.

Project 02

Recovery assessment after COVID-19 infection in flight personnel

Analyzed pre- and post-infection ECG differences and investigated associations between infection history, recovery trajectories, and key cardiovascular indicators.

Project 03

Shortcut mitigation and cross-center chest radiograph AI

Evaluated chest radiograph classifiers under institutional, device, and acquisition shifts. Developed annotation-free channel-ranking methods to retain pathology-relevant structure while suppressing non-pathological shortcuts.

Project 04

Multiscale cardiac electrophysiology and arrhythmia mechanisms

Developed ion-channel, cellular, cable, tissue, and anatomical cardiac models to study calcium-channel abnormalities, circadian regulation, drug effects, and the initiation and maintenance of cardiac re-entry.
